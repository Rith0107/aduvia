Draft — sharable card system

Step Cards

A monthly review, exported as one collectible object. The hero art is a real 14-day pulse of the user's own daily consistency — a smoothed trend curve, not a bar-per-day ledger — with a glowing marker on the most recent day. Rarity isn't a skin — Bronze through Prism Step is read directly off that month's consistency score, so a steadier curve and a higher tier arrive together, not by coincidence. Hover or focus a card for the foil tilt.

Rarity, earned by the month

Same card type — Consistency — at four real scores. The frame, the gem, and the holo strength all move together with the number.

Direction B — no card object

Atmosphere

Flighty's recap cards are the reference: no frame, no border, no icon standing in for rarity — a full-bleed gradient the data itself sets the temperature of, one oversized number, and a route line with nothing boxing it in. Same underlying rule as Step Cards — Bronze through Prism is still read directly off consistency — but the tier shows up as the light in the room, not a gem in the corner.

Same month, four moods

Bronze reads as overcast dusk; Prism as full sunrise. The 14-day pulse floats straight on the gradient, no panel underneath it.

Production notes. Step Cards drafted at display size (268×375, the real 2.5:3.5 trading-card ratio); an exported share image would render at 2–3× into a PNG sized for Instagram (1080×1512) with the tilt/hover state baked out. Atmosphere drafted at 300×533 (a scaled 1080×1920 story crop); production would render the true resolution with a heavier grain pass, since screen compression flattens fine noise. Typefaces throughout are the native Apple system stack (SF Pro Display / SF Pro Text / SF Mono) per the brief; production would swap in Aduvia's existing Manrope/Inter pairing if brand consistency with the rest of the app matters more than the system-font read. Both directions use the same underlying rarity/consistency data — picking one over the other is a call about how loud the "collectible" feeling should be, not a data or engineering difference.
